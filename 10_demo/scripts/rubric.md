# Scoring rubric: content overlap between two supplemental essay prompts

You get pairs of prompts, A and B, each with its school, its exact wording and its word limit.

Give two scores from 0 to 100, one for each direction:

- **a_from_b**: suppose a student wrote a strong essay that fully answers prompt B. What share of a strong answer to prompt A could be taken from that essay, with light editing (new opening sentence, school name swapped, a line or two added)?
- **b_from_a**: the same, the other way round.

Score content only. Ignore length: a separate step handles word limits. Do score it down when one prompt asks for something the other essay would not contain, for example a school-specific "why us" detail, a specific format (a list, a letter, a 3-word answer), or a different subject (a community vs an intellectual interest).

Anchors:

| Score | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90-100 | Same question in different words | "Why do you want to attend X?" vs "Why are you interested in X?" (school names swapped) |
| 60-80 | Same core subject; one asks for something extra | "Describe an intellectual interest" vs "What excites your curiosity, and how would you pursue it at X?" |
| 30-50 | Same raw material, different angle | "A community you belong to" vs "A time you contributed to others" |
| 10-25 | A paragraph or an anecdote could carry over | "Your intended major" vs "A challenge you overcame" |
| 0-5 | Nothing usable | "Why X?" vs "Your favourite word, in 3 words" |

"Why us" essays: the reasons a student gives for one school do not transfer to another, but the part about the student's own goals does. Two "why us" prompts usually score 40-60, not 90.

Directions differ: a broad prompt usually supplies a narrow one better than the reverse.

Score each pair independently. Do not look up other scorers' answers. Write a one-line reason for every pair.
